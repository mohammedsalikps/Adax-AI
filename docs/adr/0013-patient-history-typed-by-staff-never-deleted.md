# ADR 0013: Patient history is typed in by staff, kept forever, and read through one model

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 3 Oct 2026

## Context

A doctor consulting needs the patient's *past history*: known conditions, the medicines they take, investigation results and earlier consultations, next to the allergies and vital signs the product already held ([audit](../DOCTOR_APP_AUDIT.md) §1). None of the first three existed: history was a free-text note section, and the consultation screen showed names only. The product has no laboratory, pharmacy or other record system to take them from, so people have to type them in, and the data must still be trustworthy: who said it, when, and what was corrected.

## Decision

1. **Three tables, one change log.** `patient_conditions`, `patient_medications` and `investigation_results`, each with the entering user, the time, a `source` (`clinician_entered`: the only value for now) and a `data_origin` (`synthetic` or `clinical`, from the deployment's `DATA_ORIGIN`). `patient_history_events` records every creation and status change (who, when, from, to, why) and is append-only.
2. **Entries are never deleted and their content never changes.** A database trigger enforces it for everyone, the application role included: only the status and what explains it (and a medicine's stop date) can change. A mistake is marked **entered in error** with a reason of at least three characters; that state is final, the entry stays in the table, and it leaves the history read model. A stopped medicine is not restarted: the person enters a new one. Correcting a value is therefore "mark as an error, enter the right one".
3. **One read model.** `GET /api/patients/:id/history` returns what the consultation screen needs in one request: the active allergies with severity and reaction, conditions, the medicine list (current first), the latest results (the 30 most recent, **plus the newest result of every test**, so a long run of one test cannot push the others off the list; the rest are counted), signed consultations (never drafts or discarded drafts), and the latest value of each vital sign with its time and source (readings marked as entered in error are left out). Doctors and nurses read; administrators are refused; another facility's patient is "not found" and the attempt is audited, as everywhere.
4. **Doctors write.** `history:write` is a doctor's permission. (Allergies keep their existing route and their existing rules, so nurses can still record them.)
5. **The API sets the flag on a result**, in one place, from the number and its range (low, normal, high); for a result with no number it takes the clinician's reading (normal or abnormal), otherwise it says unknown. A flag the client sends is not trusted, and a number always wins over a reading. **The number cannot disagree with the result as it reads:** a result that is only a number ("7.2") is compared as that number whether or not one was sent, and a number that differs from it is refused (a "96" shown with the flag for 960 would mislead). Text that is not plain ("7.2 %", "<5", "Positive") is not compared; the screen therefore has no separate number field and takes the value and its unit separately.
6. **The medicine list is not the prescriptions.** The list says what the patient takes; earlier prescriptions are shown separately and labelled "what was prescribed, not a record of what the patient takes". Nothing is copied from a prescription into the list without a person doing it.
7. **No duplicates by accident.** Partial unique indexes refuse the same open condition, the same current medicine (name, strength, dose, frequency) and the same final result (test, date, value) twice, so a double click or two people entering the same thing cannot create them.
8. **Audit records ids and statuses, never the clinical text.** Reading the history is audited with counts; writing, with the entry's id and the status change.
9. **Origin on patients.** `patients.data_origin` (`unspecified` for older records, `synthetic` or `clinical` for new ones; records in the seeded synthetic facility were backfilled as synthetic) lets the screens say a patient is synthetic from the data, not only from a banner. **An entry takes its origin from its patient** (the deployment's setting only when the patient's is `unspecified`), so a record can never be labelled differently from the patient it belongs to. Because the setting defaults to `synthetic`, **a production deployment must state `DATA_ORIGIN` explicitly** (the server refuses to start otherwise); an unset variable must not label real records as synthetic.
10. **The database also refuses the quiet ways round.** Migration 0024 (written after review, because 0023 was already applied to the development database): a medicine marked as entered in error keeps the stop date it had (no date appears or changes on the way to "error"), and `TRUNCATE` of the three entry tables is refused by trigger for everyone including the superuser (the disposable test databases are reset with the trigger-skipping replication role, which the application and the demo seed never use). Free text containing a NUL character is refused with a message by the contract (PostgreSQL cannot store one), and dates before 1900 are refused.
11. **The change log is written by the API, not the database.** Each route writes its `patient_history_events` row in the same transaction as the change, so a change and its record are one unit. A direct database edit by the owner or superuser would not write one; the content-immutability and append-only triggers are what protect the record against that, and nothing here claims more.

## Consequences and limits

- **Everything is typed by staff.** There is no laboratory or pharmacy feed, no import, no coded terminology (the ICD-10 code is format-checked only, as for diagnoses), and no verification of a result against a source document. The screens say so.
- **No reconciliation workflow.** There is no "reviewed with the patient today" step for the medicine list, no interaction checking, and no ordering or grouping of conditions beyond date.
- **Only doctors write.** A nurse cannot add a condition, medicine or result (they can record allergies and vital signs). This is a decision to confirm.
- **Results are dated in calendar days on the facility's clock;** there is no time of day for a result.
- **An entry cannot be edited, only replaced.** That is deliberate (the record's trail stays honest) and costs one extra step to fix a typo.
- **Allergies are still mutable** (`updated_at`, deactivate) and are not in the change log; bringing them under the same rules is a separate change.
- **Verified by tests, not by clinical review:** the database rules, permissions, validation, flags, duplicates, audit and the read model are covered by API and database tests; whether the history screens suit a clinician's workflow has not been evaluated with clinicians.
