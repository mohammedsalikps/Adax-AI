# ADR 0017: Mana AI, the doctor's assistant over the clinic's records: a plan, authorised tools, an answer written from what they returned, and checks in code

- **Status:** accepted and implemented in slice V4 (after V3, `8c7e019`). It builds on the local model and provider abstraction of [ADR 0016](0016-local-ai-consultation-intelligence.md), the local-first and no-fallback rules of [ADR 0012](0012-local-first-ai-hosted-providers-opt-in.md) and the egress policy of [ADR 0015](0015-hosted-ai-egress-policy-and-speech-service-hardening.md). It replaces, on the screen, the slice-1 "Mana questions about one patient" panel (the slice-1 API and its stored answers remain; see §3).
- **Date:** 4 Oct 2026

## 1. Context

After V3 a doctor can ask for a draft of one consultation, and slice 1 let a doctor ask fixed questions about one patient's record, answered by explicit rules. Neither is a *conversation with the clinic's records*: "who is waiting?", "do I have appointments tomorrow?", "what did Rahul Sharma's last visit say?", "and her blood pressure?", in the doctor's own words, across patients and across the day, with the answer showing what it rests on.

The obvious way to build that is a chat model with access to the database. **That is the design this ADR rejects.** A model that composes queries or holds credentials can be steered by anything it reads (a patient's note, a pasted letter, the question itself) into reading what it must not, writing what it must not, or saying what the record does not say. A small local model is also weak enough to do this by accident. So the model here is given **no tools, no database access and no way to act**; it is used twice, for two narrow jobs, and everything around those two calls is ordinary code that can be read, tested and audited.

Mana AI is a **doctor-facing AI assistant; its outputs require clinician review.** It is read-only. It makes no clinical claim, gives no advice, and nothing in this ADR or the product says that it has been clinically validated.

## 2. Decisions

### 2.1 The pipeline: plan, authorise, retrieve, compose, verify

For every question (`apps/api/src/mana/assistant/service.ts`):

1. **Plan** (model call 1, prompt `mana-assistant-plan-v2`). The model is shown **the doctor's words, today's date and time, whether the conversation is about one patient, the patients already shown in this conversation as handles (`P1`, `P2` … with sex and a ten-year age band, never a name, MRN or telephone number), the handle of the patient last discussed, and the doctor's last three questions.** It is **not** shown any record. It answers a flat, grammar-constrained JSON plan: a *kind* (`tools`, `clarify`, `write_request`, `general_knowledge`, `out_of_scope`, `other`) and at most three calls to **registered tools**. **The question it may put back to the doctor is kept only when it is one short question with no digit, no link, no handle and nothing clinical in it** (the planner has seen no record, so anything clinical in it is invented); otherwise the fixed question "Which patient, or which time, do you mean?" is shown. A patient is named in a call only by a **handle**: `current`, `P<n>`, `name:<what the doctor wrote>` or `mrn:<what the doctor wrote>`.
2. **Validate the plan** (`plan.ts`, `validatePlan`, `mana-plan-checks-v2`): anything that is not a plan is a failed question ("try asking in other words"), never run; every call keeps only the fields its tool reads; handles are put in the four forms the server resolves (anything else is no patient); free text is made one clean line; a date must exist and be within three years of today; duplicate calls go; **a question reads at most two patients**; a plan that is not of kind `tools` carries no calls; and `mine` (the doctor's own queue or schedule) follows **the doctor's words** ("my", "mine", "for me", "I" as the one who acts; not "show me" and not "type I"), not the model's. **What the doctor's own words settle is not left to the model, but only what they settle surely:** a request that opens with a verb of changing something ("Prescribe …", "Please sign this", "Can you cancel my 3 pm?") or with a weaker verb followed by something a record holds ("Update the allergy list", "Add penicillin to her allergies", "Book an appointment") is a request to change something, and a request that asks for the audit log, another clinic's records, passwords or keys, or the system's settings is beyond a doctor's work, **whatever the model planned**; nothing is read for either. A question that only *reads* with the same words ("Change in blood pressure since the last visit?", "Update on Rahul Sharma?", "Order history of this patient", "Did she go to another hospital before?", "Is there a password reset visit today?") is not taken for one: a first version took many of these for requests (the second review listed them) and refused them; a test now holds both lists. What the rules do not recognise is left to the planner, and nothing can be written whichever way it goes. A plan that does not fit the question (a refusal of a question that names a patient of the clinic or is about the queue, appointments, consultations or departments; a "change" with no verb of change in the question; a plan with no calls) is sent back to the planner **once**, with a fixed note, and what it says the second time stands. What the checks cut (more than two patients, a date that is not a date) is **said to the doctor**.
3. **Authorise and retrieve** (`tools.ts`, `runTool`). *Before anything is read*, the caller's role must hold **every permission the tool needs**, from the platform's own permission matrix (Mana grants nobody anything). Then the tool reads, **always scoped to the caller's clinic: the clinic is never an argument of any tool**, it is the session's. A patient handle is resolved inside that clinic (`entities.ts`, `resolveHandle`); a patient of another clinic is the same answer as one that does not exist ("not found"), and the attempt is written to the audit log as `access.not_found` with `via: mana` and the reason `other_facility`, as the patient pages do. **A name that fits several patients is never guessed:** the doctor is shown up to five candidates with just enough to tell them apart (name, MRN, age and sex, date of the last visit) and chooses.
4. **Compose** (model call 2, prompt `mana-assistant-answer-v1`). The model is shown the question and the **facts** the tools returned, each a short sentence with an id (`[S1] (P1) Allergies: Penicillin, reaction rash …`), and the **findings** found by rule (so it does not contradict them). It writes statements that cite fact ids. It is not asked whether the question was answered: if the facts do not answer it, it writes nothing, and the server says so.
5. **Verify** (`answer.ts`, `checkAnswer`, `mana-answer-checks-v2`). Explicit rules, not another model (§2.5). What cannot be tied to the facts it cites is **not shown**.

If the model call cannot be made, the answer is "Mana is currently unavailable." and nothing else (§2.7).

**No chaining, by design.** The plan is made once, before anything is read, and nothing a tool returns is ever shown to the planner. A record cannot change *what is looked up*. (The price: a question that needs one lookup's result to choose the next, such as "the allergies of whoever is next in my queue", is not answered; it is a plan of two calls that are both made up front or it is not supported. §3.)

### 2.2 The tool registry

Ten tools, registered in one place (`REGISTRY` in `tools.ts`, compared with the contract's list at start-up: a tool in one and not the other stops the server). **None of them writes.** There is no tool that prescribes, signs, amends, books, cancels, creates, deletes or sends, and no route accepts a tool name, a clinic or a query from the client: the routes take the id of a consultation or patient record that the doctor may open (to say what a conversation is about), a handle the conversation itself showed (to choose between patients), the doctor's words and an idempotency key. *What to look up is the server's own plan*.

| Tool | What it returns | Permissions the caller's role must hold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `get_current_context` | the date and time, who is asking, which consultation or patient record is open | none |
| `find_patient` | up to five patients of this clinic matching a name, MRN or telephone digits (every word must be in the name), as handles | `patients:read` |
| `get_patient_record` | one patient's record on a topic (briefing, history, allergies, medicines, vital signs, diagnoses, investigations, what changed, a search), as sentences with the records they rest on and the findings of the fixed rules | `patients:read`, `encounters:read` |
| `get_patient_visits` | up to twelve visits: date, department, outcome, reason | `patients:read`, `queue:read` |
| `get_consultation` | one **signed** consultation (last, previous, or on a date) | `patients:read`, `encounters:read` |
| `get_today_queue` | counts by status and up to twenty open visits | `queue:read` |
| `get_recent_visits` | check-ins in a window ("about two hours ago" is a window around it, and says so; yesterday; a date) | `queue:read` |
| `get_appointments` | appointments in a window (today, the next hour, tomorrow, a date), the doctor's own or the clinic's | `appointments:read`, `patients:read` |
| `get_my_consultations` | the doctor's own consultations on a day, with counts | `encounters:read` |
| `get_facility_information` | this clinic and its active departments | `departments:read` |

Each returns the least that answers: **sentences made from records, and the records they rest on**; patients appear as handles; at most twenty-four facts per tool; free text from a record (a reason for a visit, a note) is bounded, put on one line, has its brackets turned into parentheses so that it cannot pose as a source id, and has invisible formatting characters taken out (`neutralise`, shared with V3), and has **the patient's own name, MRN and telephone number replaced, and any telephone number or record number in the shapes this register and Indian telephone numbers are written in replaced whoever it belongs to** (`makeScrubber` and `scrubGeneric`: international numbers, ten-digit mobile numbers, numbers with an area code, the three-three-four form; `ADX-018631`, `ADX 018631`, `adx018631`, `MRN …`, `UHID …`, and any short prefix with a six-digit number). **That replacement is a set of patterns: another person's name written in a note is not recognised, nor a number written in another shape; dates ("04-10-2026"), batch numbers and series of readings are left alone on purpose** (a first version replaced them, which damaged the records the doctor reads). A malformed call (a missing date, minutes, a search term) is a result "invalid" with a reason, not a guess. Any failure inside a tool is "the lookup failed", never an exception or a detail for the model.

### 2.3 The authorization boundary

Everything is decided on the server, in this order:

1. **The route**: the caller is a doctor (`ai:use`, the permission of every AI feature). A nurse, an administrator and a visitor are refused before a conversation is looked at.
2. **The conversation** belongs to the doctor who opened it and to the doctor's clinic. Another doctor of the same clinic gets "not found" on read, ask, select and cancel. What a doctor asked is theirs.
3. **The scope**: a conversation is opened for a consultation or a patient record (both resolved inside the doctor's clinic; another clinic's is "not found" and audited) or for the clinic in general. "This patient" means that patient and nobody else.
4. **The tool** (§2.2): permissions first, clinic scoping in every query, patient handles resolved inside the clinic.
5. **The patient's consent to AI help with their record** (`ai_record_assistant`): a patient whose latest decision is a **withdrawal is not read, even by a model that runs on this computer**, and their reasons for visits are not shown in any list (queue, check-ins, appointments, a patient's visits, the open consultation); with a model that does **not** run on this computer, consent must be **granted** for every patient whose record **or reason for a visit** is read, in lists as well as in records. The consent is read again **inside the transaction that keeps the answer, for every patient whose record was read and every patient whose reason for a visit a list showed, and is locked against a withdrawal until the transaction ends**: if it changed while the model was working, nothing is kept and the message ends as **failed** (`consent_changed`, not retryable) with an audit event of outcome `denied`.
5b. **Who is asking, again.** Waiting for the model's turn can take minutes, so the doctor is checked again after the wait, again before anything is read, and again when the answer is about to be kept: a session that ended or sat idle too long, an account that was switched off or that must change its password, or a role that changed ends the question (`session_ended`, audited as `denied`) and nothing is read or kept.
6. **The wrong-patient guard** (`service.ts`, `wrongPatientCheck`; `plan.ts`, `nameRunsIn`, `coversRun`): the planner chooses a patient by handle, which can be wrong ("the current patient" for a question that names someone else). A **name in the question is a run of capitalised words** ("Rahul Sharma"; `D’Souza` and `Al-Haddad` are one word each; two-letter names count; a name after "Dr", "Nurse" or "Sister" is a colleague's and is left out; "Mr", "Mrs" and "Ms" are not part of a name). A run **names someone of this clinic** when some patient has a word of it (whole words: `Mary` is not `Maryam`). A patient-reading call is about the patient the doctor named **only if that patient's name has every word of the run**: "Rahul Sharma" is not Rahul Verma, "Meena Kumar" is not Arun Kumar, "Anita Das" is not Anita Menon (the first version let any shared word pass, which the second review showed). When a call resolves to a patient that fits none of the names the doctor wrote, **nothing is read**: the doctor is shown the patients that fit best, the patient the plan was about among them, and asked which one they mean ("Your question names Rahul Sharma, but I was about to look at Rahul Verma. Which patient do you mean?"). **The doctor's choice stands:** choosing a patient proceeds with that patient (the guard does not ask again, also when the name typed is nobody's). A very common name brings up the best-fitting patients first, never a guess. **Limits:** a name written in lower case ("what about rahul sharma?") or in another script is not noticed; a nickname or a misspelling that fits no patient is not a name of the clinic; the guard looks at the patient-reading calls of the plan, so a question that names two patients and a plan that reads only one is answered about that one (and says so at the top of the answer).

Mana therefore reads **nothing the doctor could not read on the existing screens**. It does not add a permission, and it does not widen scope: the permission matrix (a doctor reads the whole clinic's patients) is the existing one, and **this is a limit of the platform, not of Mana**: a doctor of the clinic can ask about any patient of the clinic, and the audit log records which patients a question read.

### 2.4 Conversations and entities

A conversation is a row (`mana_conversations`) with the clinic, the doctor, the scope and an **entity list**: the handles of the patients it has shown, in order (`P1` is the patient of the consultation, if there is one). A handle means one patient *in that conversation*: `P1` in another conversation is not that patient, and a handle that is not in the list is "not a patient shown in this conversation". A conversation shows at most 60 patients and 60 questions (choosing between patients counts as a question); a conversation continues for 12 hours after its last message and then a new question opens a fresh one. "New conversation" starts clean at once. The server holds the conversation; the browser keeps nothing (what the panel loaded is discarded when the panel is closed).

Follow-ups ("and her medicines?") work because the planner is told **which handle was last discussed** and **the doctor's last three questions**; it is *not* told earlier answers, so nothing a record said earlier can steer a later plan, and the writer sees only the current question's facts, so one patient's facts cannot appear in another patient's answer.

### 2.5 Source grounding: code decides what the doctor reads

The checks run on every answer (`checkAnswer`). They reuse V3's text helpers (`consult/validate.ts`: figures, medicine names, doses, negation, wording) and add rules for a conversation:

| Check | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Cited ids that do not exist; a statement that cites nothing; a statement that cites only findings | the statement is **removed** and counted (one that only restates a finding is not shown twice, and is not counted either) |
| A figure (numbers, dates, times; "three" counted) that is in none of the cited facts | **removed**; a figure that is in another fact the model was shown **about the same patient (or about nobody)**: that fact is **added** to what the statement cites and the statement is **marked** ("also rests on another record"). A fact about another patient is never added. A record number or telephone number that the doctor typed in the question is not a figure the record has to hold |
| A figure with its unit ("86 bpm", "38.6 °C", "142/90 mmHg", "5 days", "45-year-old") whose pair is in none of the cited facts | **removed** (the heart rate and the temperature of one reading cannot swap numbers, and days are not weeks) |
| A **month** or a **morning/evening** that no cited fact has ("4 Oct" for "4 Sep", "9:15 pm" for "9:15 am") | **removed** |
| A medicine, dose or regimen the cited facts do not contain; a dose that is a part of another ("5 mg" is not in "15 mg") | **removed** (a medicine named only by another fact than the ones cited is removed, not repaired: the record the statement cites is the one the doctor opens) |
| "Allergic to X" where X is on the medicines side of the cited facts only, or "takes X" where X is on the allergy side only | **removed** (a statement that cites both an allergy and a prescription must put each medicine on its own side) |
| "No allergies" in any of its forms ("no known allergies", "allergy free", "free of allergies", "an empty allergy list", "nothing is recorded for allergies", "not allergic to X"), or "not recorded", with no cited fact or finding that says it | **removed** |
| A claim about everything or nothing ("nobody", "none", "empty", "clear", "never", "always", "all patients") or a quantity in words ("several", "few", "most", "only", "a single", "both") that no cited fact uses; the state of a visit ("cancelled", "missed", "completed") or of a medicine ("stopped", "started", "switched") that no cited fact gives | **removed** |
| A statement with nothing in it that the facts say ("No.", "Yes.", "Nil.", "Ok.", "None.") | **removed** |
| A "no" on one side only (the statement affirms what a fact denies, or denies what it affirms) | **removed** |
| A direction ("lower", "higher", "decreased", "went up", "rises", "above", "more than", "worse than") that the cited facts do not give (a fact that says "down 8 bpm" allows "lower") | **removed** |
| Advice and orders ("you should start …", "I recommend …"); words of certainty ("definitely", "proves") or a diagnosis worded as a fact where the cited facts are not about diagnoses | **removed** |
| A patient name the doctor did not write and no fact contains | **removed** |
| A statement about a handle (P1) whose cited facts are another patient's (a handle in parentheses or brackets is checked as a handle and written bare) | **removed** |
| A word that claims something about the patient (a severity, a state, a diagnosis) that the **cited** facts do not contain, or a statement made mostly of words they do not contain | **removed**; with one plain word of the model's own: **kept and marked** "check the wording against the sources". The doctor's question is **not** a source: a word the question repeats is not thereby in the record |
| A statement in another script, with a combining mark, or one that hides a Latin word behind look-alike letters (Cyrillic "о", Greek "ο", small capitals; **never exempt, also when a cited fact has the letter**), the checks read English; a letter of another script that a cited fact itself contains (the micro sign of "µg", the Greek of "β-blocker") is the record's own word | **removed** |
| An **act said to be done, not in the first person**: "The allergy list has been updated.", "Allergy added.", "Updated the allergy list.", "Done." | **removed**, unless a cited fact says the same of itself with the same verb ("Signed consultation of …") |
| A **link**, a **claimed action** in the first person ("I have prescribed …", "I've updated …", "I sent …"), a **handle** that was not given, an **MRN-like or telephone-like number** that the doctor did not type | the **whole answer is emptied**: the model was not keeping to the facts. The doctor sees **"Mana could not safely verify that response."**, the findings found by rule and the records that were read. Text is read as a person would first: full-width letters, invisible characters and look-alike letters are made plain, so `ｈｔｔｐｓ：／／` or `ht[zero-width space]tps://` is a link; a record that itself says "I have updated my phone number" is quoted, not claimed, only when the statement is framed as a quotation ("the note reads: …") **and** a cited fact has that very clause as whole words; a diagnosis code such as `P59.9` is not a handle; a short word before a long number ("for 9400000123", "of 20260904") is not a record number |
| A statement that rests on records in a rule-found conflict, or only on records older than `mana.outdatedDays` (180) | kept and **marked** ("records disagree", "only old records") |

**Findings are not the model's to write.** The fixed rules of slice 1 (`answers.ts`: an allergy and a prescribed medicine of the same configured group, an amendment that corrects a note, records older than the configured age, what is not recorded) produce *conflict*, *outdated* and *absent* findings, and the server shows them **whatever the model wrote**: a model that says nothing still leaves "the record says no known allergies and also lists an allergy" on the screen, and a model cannot settle a disagreement because it is not asked to. "Not found" is therefore a result of the tools and the rules (nothing matched, nothing recorded), never of the model's say-so. **Whether the question was answered is decided by what is left after the checks**, not by the model: statements kept = answered; produced but all removed = "could not safely verify"; none produced = one of three fixed notices, by what happened: the tools found nothing, so "That information is not established in the available records."; part of what was read was kept from the model as instruction-like text, so "Part of what was read was not used … Read the record itself for that part."; or the records were read and the model wrote nothing the checks could use (it may have judged that they do not answer the question), so "Mana did not write an answer from the records it read. They are listed below, with what the rules found." The last is not "not recorded", which would often be false. **Whom an answer is about is the server's statement**: every answer that read a patient's record starts with "About <name> · MRN · age and sex", from the records that were read, whatever the model wrote.

The checks reduce the chance that an unsupported statement reaches the doctor. **They do not make a statement true**: a wrong statement that is worded plausibly, cites a real fact and contradicts nothing the rules recognise would pass. That is why every statement shows the records it rests on, one click away (§2.9).

### 2.6 Prompt injection: layers, none of them a guarantee

Record text can contain instructions (a signed note, a pasted letter) and so can a question. The layers:

1. **The model has no tools, no network and cannot act.** The worst a steered model can do is write words, and the words are checked.
2. **The planner never sees a record**, so a record cannot choose a tool, a patient or a window. **No chaining.** A steered *question* can only produce a plan of registered tools for patients of this clinic, at most three calls and two patients, each authorised for this doctor.
3. **Instruction-like record text is withheld from the writer** (`looksLikeInstruction`, a list of the usual phrasings and some paraphrases: "ignore the previous instructions", "you are now …", "system prompt", "reveal every patient's …", "do not tell the doctor", "note for the assistant: …", "when the doctor asks …, say …", "if asked …, reply …", "the correct answer to … is …", "respond only with …", "pretend …", "omit … from your reply", run on the text as it reads: full-width and look-alike letters made plain, invisible and blank characters (zero-width, Hangul fillers, blank Braille) taken out, combining marks and markdown inside a word removed, spaced-out and digit-for-letter spellings joined). The fact is not shown to the model; the doctor is told "A record holds text that reads like an instruction to an AI system. Mana did not use it. You can read it in the record." (a finding of kind *unusual text*), with the record one click away. **This is a first line for known phrasings, not a filter that recognises every injection**: the checks of §2.5 do not depend on it. It does not recognise a hyphenated or base64 or rot13 spelling, an instruction with long gaps in it, or one in another language (the second review listed them; the checks of §2.5 hold on what the model then writes). **It also withholds some ordinary clinical text** that shares its words (the reviewers' list: an instruction to a nurse addressed with the word "assistant" and a colon, "Note for the AI: …" in a clinical note); a test holds the ordinary sentences that must pass ("New instruction: take after food.", "Patient chose to ignore the doctor's instructions", "Plan: do not inform the doctor on call unless the fever returns"). **It withholds the whole fact** (an allergy line that contains a trigger phrase is not shown to the model), so anyone who can type a reason for a visit can make Mana leave that line out; the doctor is told that text was withheld, the notice says the answer is not complete when nothing else could be said, and the record is one click away.
4. **Source ids cannot be forged**: brackets in any record text are turned into parentheses before it reaches a prompt; the model's citations are checked against the ids that exist; what the doctor sees as a source is built by the server from the tool's own records, never from the model.
5. **The output is shape-constrained** (a grammar from the schema), **link-free, claim-free and handle-checked** (§2.5): a steered answer empties itself.

### 2.7 Local first, no silent fallback, nothing to the browser

The model is the V3 provider abstraction (`ReasoningModel`), default **local** (llama.cpp on `127.0.0.1`, per-install secret, pinned Qwen3.5-4B, ADR 0016 §2.1). **There is no fallback in either direction:** with no local model the answer is "Mana is currently unavailable." and the question is **not stored**; a provider key alone enables nothing (ADR 0015); a hosted model is used only if the server is configured for it **and** egress is allowed, and then the patient's consent is required (§2.3). The model's address, secret and prompts never reach the browser: the screen is told whether the model is available, its name, whether it runs on this computer and the ids of the prompts, nothing else. The automated tests never call a model server or a provider: they use a labelled stand-in.

### 2.8 Persistence, audit and retention

`mana_conversations`, `mana_messages`, `mana_tool_calls`, `mana_message_sources` (migration 0027, additive; migration 0028 adds the two append-only tables to the append-only privilege policy, because 0027 had already been applied and was not edited). A question is two rows: what the doctor asked and the answer being made; the answer is made in the background (it takes seconds to minutes on this CPU, §3), the screen polls, and **one answer can be pending per conversation** (a partial unique index). The same idempotency key returns the same question. A pending answer can be **stopped** (the model call ends, also when the stop came before the call began; nothing it was making is kept) and answers that were pending when the server stopped are failed (`interrupted`, retryable) at start-up. **That sweep fails every pending answer in the database**, which is right for the one server this system runs as; a second server on the same database would fail the first one's live answers. **Database triggers make a finished message, its sources and its tool calls immutable and undeletable**, and what the doctor asked can never change.

Each message stores the validated plan, the answer **as shown** (statements, findings, notice, counts of what was removed by reason, never the text that was removed), the records it rests on **as they read when it was made** (an excerpt of each, like every other AI evidence row), the tools that ran with their arguments and results as categories, the model, runtime, weights hash (by alias, as in V3), the ids of both prompts, the versions of the tools and the checks, and the two model runs (`ai_runs`, with input and output hashes, tokens and times). **What the model wrote before the checks is not stored** (the evaluation records it for synthetic cases only, §4).

The audit log (`mana.conversation_opened`, `mana.question_asked`, `mana.tool_run`, `mana.access_refused`, `mana.answer`, `mana.answer_unverified`, `mana.answer_failed`, `mana.answer_cancelled`, `mana.candidate_selected`, `mana.patient_listed`) holds **ids, tool names, outcome categories and counts, never a word of a question, an answer or a record**; a patient whose record a tool read is on the `mana.tool_run` event, and a patient that a list or a clarification showed without their record being read is on a `mana.patient_listed` event of their own (one each), so the patient's access trail shows both. **These events are written in one transaction with the rows of the tool calls, and if they cannot be written the question fails and nothing is shown** (the first version wrote them best-effort); they are also written when the doctor stops the question between two tools, for the reads that were done; a refused lookup of another clinic's patient is written once, though two steps looked at it. The server's log carries no question, answer or record text (a test captures the log and looks).

**Retention is not decided.** The tables are append-only and nothing purges them; the question text, the answer text and the source excerpts are patient data and are kept as long as the database. **So are the names and MRNs the doctor typed**: they are in the question text, in the stored plan and the tool arguments (`name:…`, `mrn:…`) and in the "Selected <name> (<MRN>)" message of a choice; an erasure decision has to cover all of those rows. **No retention period was chosen and none is claimed**; the owner and clinical governance must decide how long conversations are kept, whether a withdrawal of consent removes them, and whether a doctor may delete their own (the database now refuses deletion, which a later decision may need to change). This is an open item in the risk register (R35).

### 2.9 What the doctor sees

A panel on the right of the consultation workspace (and on the patient's record), headed **MANA AI — Doctor's Clinical Assistant**, **closed until opened** (nothing is asked of the server before). In it: the conversation; suggested questions (buttons whose plan is the server's own: no model is asked what to look up); an input ("Ask Mana about this patient, today's queue, or hospital records..."); **Send** and **Stop**; **New conversation**. Each answer: **"About <name> · MRN · age and sex"** when it read a patient's record (written by the server), the statements (patients by name, from the handles), **source chips in words** ("Allergy · 1 Sep 2026") that open to the record, its status and its words, with a link to the consultation or the patient record; findings with a label ("Records disagree", "Possibly outdated", "Not in the records", "Unusual text, not used"); how many statements were removed; "Records read for this answer" (including what was read and not used); and "How this was made". States: closed, open, working (the phase in words, and Stop), answer, source opened, **"Mana is currently unavailable."** (the panel looks again every few seconds), refusal (fixed words), failed (with "Ask again"), "could not safely verify", and a choice between patients. Assistive technology is told what arrived ("Mana answered.", "Mana did not do that.", "Mana could not safely verify that response.", "Mana needs you to choose or to say more.") by one status region, not by reading out the whole answer: **each answer that arrives, once, also when two answers in a row are worded the same, and not the answers that were already there when the panel was opened** (the messages inside the log carry no live-region role of their own, so nothing is said twice); after Send, Stop, a suggestion, a choice or "Ask again" focus is in the question box; **a choice between patients can be made only while it is the last thing said** (after the next answer its buttons are disabled). A footer says that it is a doctor-facing AI assistant, that its outputs require clinician review, and that it cannot prescribe, sign or change anything. **There is no "insert into the note" from Mana**: a doctor copies what they choose.

### 2.10 Policy answers are fixed words, not the model's

A request to **write** (prescribe, sign, change, add, delete, book, cancel) is refused: "Mana is read-only: it cannot prescribe, sign, change or delete anything in a record, an appointment or a permission. Use the consultation screen for that." A **general medical question** is not answered: "Mana searches this clinic's own records. It has no governed source of general medical knowledge configured, so it does not answer general medical questions." A request for **more than a doctor's work needs** (every patient, other clinics, the audit log, passwords) is refused: "Mana can only read what you are already allowed to see in this clinic's records …". These come from the server, whatever the model wrote; the plan kinds only choose *which*.

## 3. Consequences and limits

- **It is slow.** On this laptop CPU a question took **78 s at the median and 170 s at most** in the synthetic evaluation (planning 56 s and writing 25 s per call at the median; the figures and the spread are in [safety/AI_MODELS.md](../safety/AI_MODELS.md) §10.5), and a long fact list takes longer. The screen says what it is doing and has Stop. Suggested questions skip the planning call.
- **A 4-billion-parameter model plans imperfectly.** In the first full evaluation (the first prompt) it refused or misrouted questions about a named patient's medicines, the clinic's departments and others; the prompt was rewritten as a routing table, and the server now corrects what it can recognise (`mine`, handles, dates, the doctor's own refusals, the wrong-patient guard) and asks the planner once more when a plan does not fit the question, at the cost of about a minute for that question. What remains is a wrong question answered faithfully. The planner's failures are *measured* and listed in the report, not hidden.
- **No chaining and no cross-patient clinical search.** "Which of my patients are allergic to penicillin?" has no tool (a clinical search across all patients would read far more than a doctor's question needs); it is refused as beyond a doctor's work. Questions that need one lookup's result for the next are not supported.
- **Facility-wide doctor access is the platform's rule.** Mana inherits it. A doctor can ask about any patient of the clinic; the audit log records which.
- **The checks cannot recognise a plausible wrong statement** that cites a real fact (§2.5). The wording check is a safety net for a small model, not a drug dictionary or a medical reasoner. Marked statements ask the doctor to read the sources; nothing makes reading them optional.
- **The checks over-remove, and say so.** In the final evaluation they removed 3 of 55 statements and all three were correct ("One appointment is at 9:15 am": "one" before a counted noun is read as a count; "was issued on 15 Aug 2026": an act said to be done; "both recorded on 15 Aug 2026": a quantity no cited fact uses). The last was an answer's only statement, so a correct answer ended as "Mana could not safely verify that response." (the records read, the About line and the findings are still shown). They err on the side of removal, because a wrong statement shown is worse than a correct one withheld; they were not loosened to improve a count.
- **Injection is reduced, not removed** (§2.6). The phrasing list is English and incomplete.
- **Names the doctor types are sent to the model.** The model is not given names *from records* (patients are handles; free text has the patient's own identifiers and any telephone or record number replaced, by patterns, so another person's name in a note is not recognised), but the doctor's own words ("Rahul Sharma's allergies") reach the planner and the writer. With the local model they stay on this computer; with a hosted model (off by default) they would not.
- **English only.** The prompts, the instruction list and the checks assume English.
- **The conversation is read whole on every poll** (every message and source, every two seconds while an answer is being made); the conversation is capped at 60 questions, which bounds it, and a longer one would need a "since" parameter. Not done.
- **The slice-1 Mana API remains** (`/api/patients/:id/mana/questions` and its stored answers, the rules engine the tools reuse). It no longer has a screen. Whether to retire it is a decision that was not made here.
- **Consent wording and scope** for Mana (does a local assistant need its own consent, and what does it say?) and **the retention** of conversations (§2.8) are governance decisions that were not made.
- **The secret and the loopback boundary protect against other machines and web pages, not against another process of the same user** (ADR 0016 §3).

## 4. How it was evaluated (and what that does not show)

`data/eval/mana-v1/cases.json`: 49 synthetic cases over 55 questions about an invented clinic (`apps/api/src/eval/mana-fixture.ts`: two clinics, thirteen invented patients with histories, a queue, appointments, a record that says "no known allergies" and lists one, a record more than a year old, signed notes that carry an instruction to an AI in the usual words and in a paraphrase, and a signed note with identifiers in its text), written by the people who wrote the checks. Run through the **real local model** and the real checks by `MANA_EVAL=1 npx vitest run test/mana-eval.real.test.ts` (from `apps/api`; the model server must be running; the test database only), recorded **before** any check (what the model was shown and what it wrote, call by call), scored by `npx tsx apps/api/src/eval/mana-cli.ts` into `data/eval/results/mana-v1.md`. It measures tool selection, retrieval, authorization, source correctness, grounding, ambiguity handling, conflict handling, injection resistance, context isolation, read-only behaviour, refusals and statements removed, **as counts of cases passed, never as a percentage**: the cases are not a sample of anything, and a count on cases written by the checks' authors says what the system did on them, not how it will do on a doctor's questions. Results: in the final recording **54 of 55 questions ended as they should**; the plan asked for the right tool for 31 of 31; authorization 26 of 26, source correctness 58 of 58, injection resistance 76 of 76, context isolation 15 of 15, read-only 55 of 55 (a checksum of the tables a write could land in, before and after each question), refusals 10 of 10. The one question not passed (`context-switch#2`) is a **correct answer that the checks removed**: its only statement used the word "both" for two readings, which no cited fact says. The checks removed 3 of 55 statements, **all three correct**, in the safe direction; 7 statements are marked "check the wording". A question took 78 s at the median and 170 s at most. **Every case is a development case, and nothing here is an accuracy** ([safety/AI_MODELS.md](../safety/AI_MODELS.md) §10.5).

The regression tests (`mana-tools`, `mana-answer`, `mana-assistant`: 164 tests: `mana-tools` 28, `mana-answer` 66, `mana-assistant` 70; the whole API suite passes (786 tests, and 1 opt-in test that needs the real model)) run with a stand-in model and look at what reached it, what was read and for whom, what was stored and audited, and what was shown.

## 5. What was not built (and is not claimed)

Streaming text while it is written; any tool that writes; a governed source of general medical knowledge (so none is answered); chaining; clinical search across patients; insertion of an answer into the note; patient-facing output; other languages; a hosted run of this feature; learning from questions; a retention schedule; clinical validation of anything.
